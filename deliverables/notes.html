Ring the Bell: notes

The idea. The cabinet is a 1.5 m column, so it plays like a fairground strength tester. A red charge climbs as high as the hit deserves against this machine's top, the level one hit in twenty reaches. It breaks today's line and friends' marks on the way, then holds while the score drops onto the plate. Height does the ranking, so the score is the only number on the glass and the queue reads the verdict first.

Decisions

  • No touch, no audio. States end on a timer, credit, strike or server event. Zones follow floor height: score above heads, replay at eye level, code at chest height.
  • The run is the unit. Groups take turns, so one code per run stays put beside a still of every hit. Anyone can scan and watch blurred, each person keeps their own hit with one tap, and nothing waits for an install.
  • Brand. Red stays the strike. Type gets three roles: Orbitron for short words, Inter for sentences, and a condensed numeral in fixed cells, 1.9 times Orbitron's cap height in the same width. The second red is deleted, duplicate steps fixed, and a dark ground plus surface, QR and motion tokens added.
  • The glass stays anonymous. Names, ranks and rivalry live on the phone.

Validate. Can the queue read who beat whom? Does the code scan from 1.7 m in glare? Does the striker look up in time for the landing? Does GO AGAIN feel neutral to a child after a weak hit?

Push back. Touch was left unfilled. At 1.5 m tall the panel is 0.42 m wide, not 0.5, and mounting height moves every band. Three decimals are likely false precision. "One attempt and a walk-away" counts the wrong unit; I would track kept hits per run.
