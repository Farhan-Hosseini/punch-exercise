The product

A physical arcade machine, installed in malls and entertainment venues, paired with a mobile app.

One credit buys one attempt. The player steps up and performs a single full-force strike against a padded target. A sensor measures it and returns a score. The attempt is over in under five seconds, and a camera records it for playback on the machine's screen.

The app is where attempts live afterwards: the player's profile, a reel of their recorded attempts and scores, and the people they're competing against.

Players are walk-up mall traffic, usually in groups, often with a queue behind them. They didn't leave the house planning to do this. Revenue is attempts: one attempt and a walk-away is a failure, three attempts and an app install is the goal.

What to design

The experience from the end of an attempt through to the player's phone. How many screens that takes is up to you. Design what the flow needs.

What it has to do:

On the machine

  • Present the score as an event, not a readout
  • Show the playback of the attempt (a placeholder is fine, don't go sourcing video)
  • Make the player want to go again
  • Get the attempt onto their phone. How that works is your call

On the phone

  • The player's profile and a reel of their attempts: score, video, when, where
  • The social layer: watching other people's attempts, reacting to them, feeling connected to the people who use these machines

Competition, optional

  • Who's the best. Which surface that belongs on is part of the question

Constraints

Arcade screen

  • 1080 × 3840 px, portrait
  • Physical panel is large, think 1.5 meters tall, 0.5 wide
  • Scores range from 0 to 999,999.000. At full length that's six digits, a separator and three decimals
  • No cursor, no keyboard, no mouse. Touch [IS / IS NOT] available, and the player has just used both hands
  • Viewed standing from 1 to 1.5 m, with spectators a further 2 to 3 m back
  • Loud ambient environment. Nothing can depend on audio
  • Runs unattended all day. Mounted, not adjustable

How this showcase answers each point

Every ask in the brief, and where it lives. Each button closes the brief and takes you there.

The goal

  • Three attempts and an installThe code on the glass installs the app while it links the phone, so the install happens before the first strike. After it, Punch again closes every result screen, and an empty wallet buys a pack of three. A payment that fails says nothing was charged and tries again with the same pack.

On the machine

  • How many screens it tookEight on the glass, in the three groups of its page bar: Home and the leaderboard under Home, Scan, Punch now and Reading under Play, and the Result, the Big score and New record under Results. Fourteen on the phone, from Home through the link, the credits and the hit to the reel, the leaderboard, the feed and the profile.
  • Between visitsThe glass rests on Home: a Monster Energy banner across the top, a welcome with the code, today’s best and how it works, over the sponsor strip at the foot.
  • The score as an eventThe glass reads the strike first, so the number arrives as news. A record then gets a moment of its own, and the result lands the score above every head with its charge. Slot reels is one of the four ways the result draws that number. The Big score holds it for the back of the queue, with whose hit it is, one reason to care and the player’s own punch on video.
  • The playbackWatch performance on the result plays the attempt with its scrubber, and Recent punches follows it: the last hits other players landed on this machine. The Big score and New record play the player’s own punch on a muted loop, each resting on its strike frame, and the same replay waits on the phone. Licensed punch videos stand in for the camera.
  • Going againPunch again closes every result screen, the Result, the Big score and New record, as the one red call on the glass. On the phone, Your hit keeps the next credit one tap away, and an empty wallet buys a pack of three.
  • Onto the phoneThe phone app’s own code, at chest height on the Scan screen, links a phone before the turn, under the player card and above the three steps that say what happens next. The phone connects and holds a credit, a Connected page names the machine it is linked to, its punch step hands the turn to the glass with our logo on it, and the hit comes back to the phone by itself. The Animation tab plays both screens together.

On the phone

  • HomeThe app opens on Home: the credits chip, the play card with its code, your last hit to replay, the machines near you and your friends’ hits, with Feeds opening the social layer.
  • Profile and reelThe profile, then a reel of your hits, each with its score, the video, and when and where it landed. The reel scrolls without end, full screen or as a grid two across, with reel settings and a three dots menu. Saving a hit is one mark on the attempt itself, so there is nothing to file and nothing to manage afterwards.
  • Your hitThe score, the replay and the strike in numbers: peak force in kg, the change on your last hit, your best, where most hits here land and hand speed when a camera can measure it, each tagged with where it came from. The glass keeps to one number, so the result leaves its kinematic breakdown switched off at rest and these live on the phone.
  • The social layerA feed of other players’ hits with stories, likes and comments, filters for Following and Nearby, and follow or challenge from any profile, so the people on the same machines feel close.

Competition

  • Who’s the bestBoth surfaces, for different jobs. The glass shows the board between players, for the queue: a score to beat before anyone steps up. After a hit the result puts the score in its place on this machine, in the city, in the country and in the world, and the Big score names the crown it took or the gap to the next mark. The phone holds the standing rivalry, global, national and Dubai, with your own row pinned wherever you are in it.

The constraints

  • 1080 × 3840 portraitEvery machine screen is drawn at that size and opens at actual size, a glass pixel to a screen pixel when the window is wide enough. Under Size on screen, Reading size fits the whole panel in the window instead.
  • 1.5 m tall, 0.5 m wideWhere everyone stands, and the band of glass each of them can read, is drawn in the case study. At that height a 1080 by 3840 panel is closer to 0.42 m wide.
  • 0 to 999,999.000Full length by default, with the three decimals smaller and quieter. The Score format control switches every surface to whole points.
  • TouchDesigned as not available. The player has just used both hands, so every state ends on a timer, a credit, a strike or a phone event, and anything that needs a finger happens on the phone.
  • Seen from 1 to 1.5 m, and 2 to 3 m backMounted and not adjustable, so every job keeps a fixed height: the score above heads for the queue, the replay at eye level for the player, the code at chest height. Big numbers are sized by cap height to fit their column.
  • No audioNothing depends on sound. The countdown is a big number in a ring that drains, the strike shows as a flash, and the phone buzzes instead of beeping.
  • Unattended all dayBetween visits the glass rests on Home, and between players it loops its leaderboard. A missed punch keeps the credit and frees the machine. Faults, a queued credit and the hours outside a run branch off the state diagram.
  • Phone platform and frameiPhone 17 Pro Max at 440 × 956 and Android at 412 × 915, opening at actual size. Switch them in Customise, under The phone.

The brand

  • What changed, and whyThe red stayed and got one job. Orbitron gave way to Big Shoulders Display, Barlow and Barlow Condensed, which hold up from three metres. The ramps became named roles, in Arena and Reference, dark and light. Reference keeps the Figma frames as drawn.
  • The sponsorMonster Energy, in its own green, in four places that never take the player’s moment: a banner across the top of the glass at Home, the strip at the foot of every screen, a banner on the phone’s Home and a post in the feed.

Across both surfaces

  • Pages built from sectionsEvery page on the phone and the glass is a stack of sections, and every section is drawn several ways, most of them five. A page bar in two steps picks a group, then a screen in it, and Customise opens on that screen’s own sections with Across the showcase beneath them, the one accordion holding everything the two surfaces share. On the phone every title and its icon button share one centre line, and the content ends one step over the tab bar.
  • One backgroundEight looks on one host of lights, shared by the phone and the glass and chosen once in Customise. Red glow is the default.
  • One QR codeEvery code, on the glass, the phone and the result, is the phone app’s own, on its glass tile.
  • Real photographs and videosA licensed library of strikes, boxers, venues and friends, each cropped from its own focal point, with the photographers credited. Every player on the boards has a shoot of their own, and no photograph is used twice. Real punch videos play on the reel, the Big score and New record, muted and looping, each resting on its strike frame.

The deliverables

  • Figma fileFrames at 1080 × 3840 for the glass and at the two phone sizes, in the file shared for this task. Both surfaces are built natively there, every layer a frame, text or vector and not one screenshot, across pages for the cover, the design system, the components, the assets, the machine screens, the mobile app and the section variants. Screens are composed from instances of the component sets, and the colours are bound to variables with a mode for Arena and Reference in dark and light.Open in Figma
  • One motion clipThe score reveal on the live glass, ten seconds, as MP4 and GIF, the glass master beside the presentation cut.
  • Half a page of notesThe decisions and why, what to validate with real players, and where I pushed back on the brief.

What you are evaluating

  • A reveal worth watchingThe glass reads the strike before it shows the number, a record gets a screen of its own, and the score lands above the queue. The Animation tab plays it beside the phone.
  • The screen it is onThe glass is laid out by who reads which band of it, from the pad and from the back of the queue, not as a phone screen stretched tall.
  • Weight and timingThe clip runs every rise and fall on one stage gravity, so the number lands instead of sliding in, then holds still long enough to read.
  • The handoffThe phone links at the scan, hands the turn to the glass with no clock of its own, and gets the hit back by itself.
  • Attention to detailArena and Reference, dark and light, reduced motion, the score format, eight backgrounds and seven logo marks all run through the same tokens. Customise switches every one of them, down to the loader that opens the site: the chosen mark, a bar and one line of type, and nothing else.

The AI note

  • Where AI was usedClaude in Claude Code audited the brief, ran directions past separate judges, built the Figma file, the reveal and this showcase, gathered and checked the photo library, and reviewed each screen. An AI panel of four expert lenses advised what the glass should leave out after the reveal, and a research pass replaced the phone’s physics readings with the strike in numbers.
