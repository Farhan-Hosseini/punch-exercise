Principles

Four rules settled most arguments while the screens were drawn. When two designs disagreed, the one that kept these won.

Red keeps one job

Red is the strike and what it earned: the score's plate or charge, its best rank, the replay's progress and the next move, Punch again. Nothing decorative is red, so from three metres back red always points at this hit.

Height does the ranking

A harder hit sits higher. The charge climbs a column on Pole, the Big score's strength tester, and the boards stand punchers on steps and bars, so a rank reads as height before anyone reads the number under it.

9

Numbers are sized by cap height

A big number asks for a cap height, then shrinks only as far as its column demands, using the measured width of one digit. The brief's longest score still fits the glass on one line.

Two distances, one set of roles

The glass and the phone share one red, one number face and one set of roles. The phone adds blur and glass for arm's length; the machine keeps solid surfaces that hold up under mall lighting.

Brand, as given and as changed

The brief's tokens were offered as a starting point. This system kept the red, replaced the type and folded the rest into named roles. The swatches are the brief exactly; the notes say what happened to each and why.

What this system did with it

  • Kept

    Red, #EB1110, as the only accent

    Primary 500 became the strike. Light appearance nudges it to #E3100F so it holds the same weight on paper.

  • Replaced

    Orbitron, with Big Shoulders Display, Barlow and Barlow Condensed

    Orbitron's wide, square digits spend the narrow 1080 glass on width, and its closed shapes blur at three metres. A condensed face gives the same column a much taller score. Measured on the 920 px column, with the brief's longest score:

  • Dropped

    Magenta, #D842D3, from the machine

    Two saturated accents fight, and on the glass red already means the charge. Magenta is free for campaigns and sponsor moments in the app, never for a state.

  • Warmed

    The neutrals, into ink and paper

    The brief's greys lean pink in the middle of the scale. Arena keeps their warmth at the ends only: #F5F3EF ink on a #0B0B0C ground in Dark, #151413 on #F3F0EB in Light.

  • Folded

    Sixteen opacity steps, into four roles

    Nobody picks white at 30% on purpose. The scales became ink 2 at 72%, ink 3 at 56%, line at 10% and track at 12%, named for what they do.

  • Fixed

    Two repeated steps in the brief

    Primary 700 and 800 are both #8D0A09, and neutral 400 repeats 300 at #CCBFBB. A scale with a repeated step can't be used by position, which is one more reason to name colours by role.

  • Tuned

    Green and red, for rank movement only

    The supportive colours keep their meaning on the phone, up and down a leaderboard, and are retuned per ground for contrast. Yellow survives only as a champion's crown on the phone. The glass is red throughout, its crowns, records and confetti included.

What I would change next, with the brand team

  • Name every colour by its job in the source tokens, so the red is strike, not primary-500.
  • Draw a condensed wordmark for the narrow glass instead of setting PUNCH APP in a typeface.
  • Test the red and both grounds on a real panel under mall light before locking values.

      The big-number rule

      Each big number asks for a cap height and takes whichever is smaller: the size that gives that cap height, or the size at which the whole number fits its column. Two tokens per theme make it work: --cap, the face's cap height as a share of its size, and --num-w, the width of one digit. A number's width is counted in digits, separators included, so a short score grows and a long one never clips.

      font-size = the smaller ofcap height ÷ --cap .8column ÷ (width in digits × --num-w .424)

                  Pages and sections

                  Every page on the phone and every screen on the glass is a stack of sections, and every section keeps a handful of designs, most of them five. Each design is its own composition, never a recolour, and the first is the one the page started with, improved. Designs that earned their place stayed; the ones that repeated a neighbour or fought the section's job were cut, so a shorter row is a decision, not a gap. Customise lists the sections of the screen on show under This screen, then one accordion, Across the showcase, for everything the two surfaces share.

                  Page
                  A phone page, .m-page[data-page], or a machine screen, .mscreen[data-mscreen]. It lays its sections out and owns the gaps between them.
                  Section
                  data-sec names it and data-sec-label titles its Customise row. It keeps its room whichever design is on show, so a change never shoves the rest of the page.
                  Design
                  A direct child with data-sv, named for what it is: Podium, Glass tile, Slant cut. The engine shows one, hides the rest and fires psec, so the page can restart its motion.
                  Shared
                  The glass's header and sponsor strip and the phone's tab bar are one choice for every page, data-sec-scope="global", listed under the page's own rows.

                  Two-step page bars, and This screen

                  Over each surface the groups sit in the shell's pill and the chosen group's screens sit in a quieter row under it. A group with one screen has no second row, and picking a group opens the screen you last had in it. Customise opens on This screen, the sections of the screen on show, and everything shared sits under it in one accordion, Across the showcase. Pick a screen here and the panel follows, as the real one does.

                                The strike in numbers

                                The glass shows one number. The fuller reading lives on the phone's Your hit, and its first version got it wrong: one pad reading in four units nobody in a Dubai mall uses, two of them impossible. Research into punch machines, punch trackers and the sports science of a punch replaced them with five numbers a player can picture and repeat, each rounded to what the pad can honestly claim and tagged with where it came from.

                                Five numbers, each with its source

                                The value beside each is read from the hit on the phone right now. Measured is a solid dot, your history a ring, this machine a bar and the camera a broken ring, so an estimate never looks like a reading.

                                1. Peak force

                                  Measured

                                  about 315 kg, 15 past the bell

                                  In kilograms, the unit the UAE uses and the one Japanese punch machines taught players to boast in. Rounded to 5 and always worded about, because a good pad reads within about a tenth. The sensor reads on past the bell, so two bell ringers still differ.

                                2. On your last hit

                                  Your history

                                  +30 kg on your last

                                  The first question every tracker answers. A change under a twentieth of the hit reads About the same, so an ordinary wobble never reads as a decline.

                                3. Your best

                                  Your history

                                  This one, a new best

                                  The one comparison that is always fair, and a reason to scan: without the app, no best is kept.

                                4. Most hits here today

                                  This machine

                                  100 to 175 kg, well above

                                  The middle half of today's hits on this pad, never an Olympian or a car crash, and one kind word for where yours sits: well above, in the middle or on the way up. It never says below.

                                  Camera estimate

                                Saving a hit

                                Keeping a hit used to mean a page of named lists to file it in. Almost nobody sorts a reel, so the lists and the page went and the save became one mark on the attempt, the same mark wherever that attempt appears.

                                • Tap

                                  Marks the attempt, the mark fills, and a toast says Saved for later. Nothing asks where it went.

                                • Tap again

                                  Takes the mark off and says so. The button is the same control both ways, so there is nothing to undo.

                                • One mark, everywhere

                                  The reel's tools, a feed post and the header of Your hit all draw the same hit, so marking it in one place fills it in the others at once.

                                • It survives a reload

                                  The marks keep to the phone in punch-saved.v2, and a blocked store only shortens their life to this visit.

                                Any button with data-save-key carries the mark with no script of its own: mpages/saved.js listens once for the whole app, writes the key and fires saved, which repaints every button drawn on that hit. window.punchSaved exposes has, save, remove and paint for a page that draws its own.
