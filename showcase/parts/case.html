A column of glass on the ground floor.

A 1080 by 3840 portrait screen, one and a half metres of glass at Dubai Mall, running all day. One credit buys one attempt: a sensor turns the strike into a score and a camera records it.

One column. One moment at a time.

Every state ends on a timer, a credit, a strike or a server event, so the Punch Machine never waits for a touch. This is the real screen, live in the page.

Between players, the leaderboard.

The queue always has a score to beat. Between visits the Punch Machine rests on Home.

Scan to connect.

A code at thumb height links a phone before the turn, so the machine knows whose hit is next.

Punch now.

The Punch Machine keeps the only clock, twenty seconds with a ring that drains. The phone shows no timer, it says look up.

Reading the strike.

Two seconds between the hit and the number, so the striker is looking up when the score lands.

The big score.

The number at full width for the back of the queue, with the punch itself looping under it.

The result.

Nine sections: the score and its ranks, the replay, the punches other people landed here, the sponsor. A record gets a red moment of its own first, and all end on the one red call, punch again.

Scan.

The code opens the app, or its download, and links the phone to this machine.

Connect, or buy credits.

Connecting spends a credit. An empty wallet opens a pack of three: Apple Pay, Google Pay, a card or PayPal. A failed payment gets a page of its own, saying first that nothing was charged.

Punch.

The phone names the machine, holds the credit, then gets out of the way. No timer here, only look up.

Your hit.

The score lands here too, with the replay, the strike at about 315 kg of peak force, and the boards it just joined: this machine, then the city and the country.

The feed, and a reel of your own.

Other people's hits to watch and react to, and your own kept ones: score, video, when and where. Kept hits start private.

The two together.

The phone hands the turn over and the Punch Machine keeps the time. The Animation tab plays them live, and holds a clip of each: the score reveal on the Punch Machine, the run on the phone, both recorded from the showcase itself.

Neither clip is a screen recording.

Each one is rendered a frame at a time from the running screens, so the timing is exactly what they play.

One system, two surfaces.

A panel read from three metres and a phone read at arm's length cannot share a type size, but they can share everything underneath it. One set of tokens, one spacing ladder, one icon family, one red.

Spacing

Twos to 12, fours to 48, eights beyond

Every margin, padding and gap on both surfaces is a step on one ladder, so a gap means the same thing wherever it appears.

Type

Big Shoulders, Barlow, Barlow Condensed

Numbers in the condensed display face so six digits fit a narrow panel; text in Barlow, which holds up small on a phone and large on glass.

Colour

One red, with one job

Red is the charge, the call and the live state. Everything else is ink on a warm dark ground, bound to variables in dark and light.

What ships is one Figma file.

The system and both surfaces are delivered in the project's Figma file, built natively: every layer is a frame, text or a vector, not a screenshot among them, and all of it on real auto layout.

Arena, the default.

Big Shoulders Display for numbers, Barlow for text, Barlow Condensed for labels, on a warm ink with one red: the charge, the replay's progress and punch again, and nothing else.

Colour is a variable.

Dark and light. Daylight turns the ground and nothing else, and no frame is redrawn for it.

One icon family.

Line icons with round caps, on the Punch Machine, in the app and on this page.

How AI was used.

Claude in Claude Code worked as a design team, and every step had a reviewer.

  • An audit, then five directions.

    Five directions scored by judges working apart, then three skeptics who reshaped the work around the run.

  • Builders on a shared contract.

    Agents built the Figma file through its API, rendered the clips from code and gathered the photo library.

  • Then a pass that only cut.

    A critic on every screen, and out went whatever repeated something already on it.
